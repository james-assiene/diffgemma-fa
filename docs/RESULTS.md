**`arg acc` is over `arg_total = 291` for every arm** — the number of arguments
the *benchmark* asked for across these 130 records. It used to be over only the
arguments of records that happened to parse, which is why `mask-sample` read
0.508 (63/120) here until 2026-08-08 and now reads 0.216 (63/291). Its numerator
never changed; 63 of its 130 records produced nothing at all and were silently
removed from its denominator and from nobody else's.

**Read the two leftmost columns together, and neither alone.**

- **CS** is the guarantee the sampler enforces: the emitted *token sequence* is accepted by the compiled automaton. That automaton carries SPEC §3.6's `<|channel>NAME\n<channel|>` header, which is **our** addition — the stock model has no reason to emit it, so the baseline arms score near zero here partly for a convention they were never asked to follow. A 0% → 100% CS jump reported on that basis alone would overstate the result.
- **schema valid** asks whether the parsed object validates against the schema. Header-independent and tokenizer-independent, so it is the question every arm can be asked fairly, and it is what the benchmark cares about.
- **arg acc** is per *argument*, over `arg_total` arguments rather than over records; **exact call** requires every argument of a call to be right and is the stricter, more comparable number.
- SPEC §3.8: CS can be trivially 100% while measuring nothing — a run emitting `{"location":""}` every time scores perfectly. That is why the content columns are never dropped.

## Rescored 2026-08-08 — what the measurement audit changed

The accuracy columns above are recomputed from the **same recorded emissions**;
no arm was re-run and no model output changed. Three scoring defects, all of the
same family — *a number computed over a denominator that depends on what the
model emitted*:

- **[A] `metrics.normalise` was not BFCL's rule.** It stripped `"` (which BFCL
  does not) and kept the space (which BFCL strips), had no single-quote fold,
  and applied the string rule to every type. BFCL normalises **only** strings
  and compares everything else exactly, after a type check. Fixed against
  `bfcl_eval/.../ast_checker.py`, which is vendored under `artifacts/data/`.
- **[B] `arg_total` dropped records that did not parse.** Fixed: it is now
  `Σ len(want)` over every record with a ground truth.
- **[B2] `arg_total` also dropped records that OOMed or hit `Z == 0`.** Same
  defect one level up, and on this corpus much larger. Fixed: those records
  reached the model and failed, so they stay in both `n` and `arg_total`.

**Every number in this section is the shipped column** — all three fixes
applied together, which is what `eval/run.py` now produces. Across the 39 BFCL
artifacts, `arg_accuracy` moves **up on 16 arms, down on 13, unchanged on 10**.
The largest movers:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `exp_e5_grammar130_j0` | **70 / 130 zero-partition** | 0.696 | **0.282** | [B2] |
| `exp_greedy_unconstrained` | 0 | 0.500 | **0.076** | [B] — 109/130 unparsed |
| `exp_e5_grammar130_s1` | **55 / 130 zero-partition** | 0.691 | **0.392** | [B2] |

`exp_e5_grammar130_j0` is the sharpest case and it was found only after [B] was
fixed: with [B] alone it *rose* to 0.732 and stayed best-in-table while having
failed to generate on 54% of its records. **A run that did not produce an answer
on more than half its records cannot report the best accuracy in the table**, and
until this fix it did.

The direction of [A] alone is worth recording separately, because it is not the
direction it sounds: it is **net favourable**, and dominated by BFCL's own
int→float acceptance. 128 comparisons went wrong→right on pairs like `133`
against a ground truth of `133.0` — the old rule deleted the decimal point and
compared `"1330"` to `"133"`. Six went right→wrong on pairs like `1000` against
`100.0`, which the old rule scored **correct** for the same reason. Isolated,
[A] moves 25 of 39 arms and never by more than +0.079. **Those isolation figures
appear nowhere in this document's tables** — they are not what the code ships.

### Did our own scoring change hand the constrained arm the lead?

The rescore moved the headline ordering in the direction we would like, and it
was authored here, so it owes a control. Decomposing the two headline arms:

| arm | stored | after [A] | after [B]/[B2] | net | [B] exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unconstrained | 0.6413 | +0.0326 | −0.0347 | **−0.0021** | 4 records / 15 args |
| constrained, ws-tolerant | 0.6285 | +0.0278 | −0.0068 | **+0.0210** | 1 record / 3 args |

**[A] is nearly symmetric** (+0.033 vs +0.028) — the int/float conversions are
not the driver. **[B] is the whole of it**, and it acts unequally not because
the rule is asymmetric — the denominator is exactly 291 for both — but because
the *exposure* is: [B] restores 4 records to the unconstrained arm's denominator
and 1 to the constrained arm's.

Those five records were read individually rather than assumed. The unconstrained
arm's four are two **empty** emissions (`live_simple_60-29-0`, `66-30-0`), one
**refusal** (`73-36-1`, *"I am sorry, but I do not have the ability to process
or analyze audio files"*), and one JSON object **truncated by the token budget**
(`106-63-0`) — so `extract_json` returning `None` is correct in all four and
there is no parser bias against unfenced output. The constrained arm's single
record is `live_simple_122-78-0`, which is **one of the two we already flag as
compiled from the defective `any`-type grammar** — our own compiler bug, and it
counts *against* the constrained arm.

**The control, and it changes the claim.** Restricting to the 125 records where
*both* arms produced a parsable call:

| | shipped (n = 130) | both parsed (n = 125) |
|---|---|---|
| unconstrained `arg acc` | 0.6392 | **0.6703** [0.613, 0.723] |
| constrained, ws-tolerant `arg acc` | 0.6495 | **0.6667** [0.609, 0.720] |
| gap | **+0.0103** | **−0.0037** |
| `exact call` gap | +0.0231 | +0.0240 |

**The `arg acc` advantage reverses sign under control.** The shipped +0.010 is a
**coverage** effect — the constrained arm fails to emit a parsable call less
often — which is a real and interesting property of constrained decoding, but it
is *not* the claim a column headed "arg acc" appears to make. On the records
both arms answered, the two are indistinguishable and the unconstrained model is
fractionally ahead. The exact-call gap does survive the control at +0.024, but
see "Paired comparisons — exact call": it is 7 discordant records against 4,
p = 0.55, and the Wilson intervals above overlap almost entirely.

**So the supported statement is "the guarantee costs nothing", in both
directions. Nothing in this cut supports "the guarantee helps".**

## SPEC §7.3 — overhead

The paper's headline performance claim, measured for the first time.

**Per denoising step**, sum-product tree in float64 at `L = 256`, against a model forward measured at 0.21 s (Phase 0):

| `\|S\|` bucket | tree | vs. one forward |
|---|---|---|
| 64 | 0.5 ms | **0.2%** |
| 128 | 2.2 ms | **1.0%** |
| 256 | 18.1 ms | **8.6%** |
| 512 | 173.4 ms | **82.6%** |

**The paper's ~4% holds only for small automata.** The crossover is sharp and lands where SPEC §5.6 predicted (`|S| ≈ 512`): below it the tree is noise against a forward pass, above it it nearly doubles the step.

> **Corrected 2026-08-08.** This paragraph previously continued: *"The whitespace-tolerant
> grammar roughly doubles `|S|`, moving most records up one bucket — so the fix that bought the
> accuracy also moved us toward the expensive end of this table."* **Both halves are wrong for the
> shipped default**, and the sign is backwards.
>
> That was measured on `PRETTY_WS` and on the superseded `JSON_WS = [ \t\n\r]{0,8}`, which did
> roughly double `|S|`. The `{0,8}` bound was never buying finiteness — it was silently a nesting
> *depth* counter costing eight states — and RFC 8259's `[ \t\n\r]*` is a Kleene star over a
> 4-character class, i.e. **one** state. Independently re-derived through lift + Valmari by the
> audit reviewer, `channel_header=False`:
>
> | whitespace | flat 2-key | +array | 3-key BFCL | renderings accepted |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | outlines stock `[ ]?` | 42 (b64) | 55 (b64) | 71 (b128) | 2/5 |
> | `PRETTY_WS` | 90 (b128) | 121 (b128) | 143 (b256) | 3–4/5 |
> | `{0,8}` (superseded) | 98 (b128) | 132 (b256) | 155 (b256) | 4–5/5 |
> | **`[ \t\n\r]*` (shipped)** | **34 (b64)** | **45 (b64)** | **59 (b64)** | **5/5, every depth** |
>
> So the whitespace-tolerant grammar is **smaller than the stock one**, and moves records **down**
> a bucket rather than up. The accuracy fix and the cost are not in tension; that framing was an
> artifact of a worse pattern.
>
> **The §7.3 bucket mix above must be re-measured under `[ \t\n\r]*` before any conclusion is
> drawn from it.** The per-bucket timings are unaffected — they are a function of `|S|`, not of
> which grammar produced it — but *which* bucket the BFCL corpus actually lands in has changed,
> and that is the quantity this table was being used to argue about.



Three things make this **not** comparable to SPEC §7.3's projection:

- `log_matmul` is no longer a GEMM. It is two fused reductions over the broadcast `A + B`, because every GEMM-shaped form underflowed on real grammars. SPEC §0's kernel-count property survives; its GEMM-throughput property does not.
- The **max-plus (MAP) tree is not measured**. Benchmarking it wedged the harness three times; `maxplus_combine` materialises `[L/2, n, k, m]`, which is 68 GB at `|S| = 512`. A real gap, not an omission for brevity.
- float32 is excluded: besides driving spurious `Z == 0` on >50% of records, it **hangs XLA:GPU** at `|S| >= 128` in the fused reduction (`|S|=64` compiles in 0.08 s; `|S|=128` never returns).

**Constrained decoding is mostly *faster* end to end, not slower.** The grammar makes the model place a stop token deliberately, while the unconstrained arm generates toward the token budget; the per-step tree cost is more than repaid by shorter generations. Treat the spread as indicative rather than tight — these are single runs on a box that saw OOM retries and preallocation changes mid-campaign.

# Cross-task results (2026-08-04)

The single-dataset story was not general. Two further datasets, chosen because
their grammars sit at the opposite end of the `|S|` scale from BFCL's JSON
schemas (bucket 64 against 128-1024), invert the conclusion.

## Sudoku 4x4, n=250, synthetic with uniqueness verified by exhaustive solve

| arm | CS | parsed | solved |
|---|---|---|---|
| unconstrained | 0.000 | 0.984 | **0.864** |
| `mask` — per-position, sampled independently | 0.004 | **0.040** | **0.012** |
| `j1` + sample | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.012** |
| `j0` + sample | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.000** |
| `j0` + MAP | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.000** |
| `j2` | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.000** |
| `j0` + MAP + 64-token scratchpad | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.004** |

**`parsed` is here because SPEC §3.8 requires it**, and on this table it is the
column that carries the story. `mask` parses **0.040** — 240 of its 250
emissions contain no 4×4 grid at all — so its `solved = 0.012` is not "it solved
1% of the puzzles", it is "it almost never produced a grid". Do not read a solve
rate without the parse rate beside it.

**Rescored 2026-08-08, `mask` from 0.204 to 0.012.** The scorer read the first
16 digits found anywhere in the emission, and `mask` emits things like
`<|channel>4312\n2134\n3241\n1423331` — three clean rows and a fourth run of
junk digits. Taking the first 16 recovered a valid grid from strings that are
not grids. The scorer now locates the grid **structurally** (four consecutive
lines of exactly four digits). Adjudication, since this moves a published
number: the compiled automaton **rejects 50 of the 51** records the old rule
scored `ok` (`CS = 0.004`), so the strict reading agrees with the grammar and
the lenient one contradicts it on 45 records; and strict leaves the clean arms
untouched (`unconstrained` 0.864 → 0.864, seed 1 0.868 → 0.860), so it is not
merely punishing headerless output. A prefix-tolerant rule was measured at 0.192
and rejected as a search rather than a rule — it has no principled stopping
point.

Seed replication (the whole finding rests on the baseline, so it was
measured twice):

| | seed 0 | seed 1 |
|---|---|---|
| unconstrained | 0.864 | 0.860 |
| constrained (MAP) | 0.000 | 0.000 |

(Seed 1 was 0.868 before the 2026-08-08 rescore; two of its 250 emissions
contain no grid and now score `no grid` instead of being reconstructed from a
digit scan. The baseline is stable across seeds either way.)

## Constraining at all is what costs, not the joint machinery

Solve rate tracks **whether the decoder is the model's own**: unconstrained
0.864 against every constrained variant at 0.000-0.012, with no meaningful
spread among the constrained variants.

> **Weakened 2026-08-08 by the scorer fix above.** This paragraph used to read
> "unconstrained 0.864 -> masking 0.204 -> joint ~0.000" and concluded that
> "masking is ~20x better than the joint methods, which implicates the joint
> machinery". **That gradient was an artifact of the first-16-digits scorer.**
> Corrected, `mask` is 0.012 against `j1`'s 0.012 — masking and the joint
> methods are indistinguishable here, and the middle of the gradient is gone.
> What survives is the outer contrast, which is large and unaffected:
> unconstrained 0.864 against **every** constrained variant at ~0.00-0.01. So
> the finding is "constraining at all is catastrophic on this grammar", not
> "the joint machinery specifically is". Nothing in this cut implicates the
> joint path over masking.

## What this is not

Three hypotheses were tested and killed, in order:

1. **The grammar removes the model's scratchpad.** No: the unconstrained model
   emits a correct grid *directly* (`2143|4321|3214|1432`) with no reasoning,
   and widening SPEC §3.6's channel header from 8 to 64 free tokens recovered
   nothing (0.000 -> 0.004). The model spent the space echoing grid rows.
2. **The grammar excludes the correct answer.** No: 45 of 58 unconstrained
   emissions match the compiled grammar *exactly* at string level (54/58 with
   trailing-whitespace slack). The right answer is in the language; the
   decoder picks a different member.
3. **MAP's product-of-marginals argmax is the damage.** No: `--emission
   sample`, which draws from the constrained posterior rather than maximising
   it, collapses identically (0.012 vs 0.000).

Also note `CS = 0.000` for the unconstrained arm is the channel-header
artifact documented for BFCL — the stock model has no reason to emit our
header — not a coverage failure.

## The revised claim

**The guarantee is free when the grammar encodes the task's correctness
condition, and can be catastrophic when it only encodes the output's shape.**
`sudoku_regex` pins the givens and the digit alphabet and says nothing about
rows, columns or boxes, so it adds no information about what makes an answer
right. For BFCL the JSON schema largely *is* the correctness condition, which
is why constraining there **costs nothing**.

That phrasing is deliberate and the arrow that used to be here — `0.639 ->
0.650` — has been removed. An arrow asserts a direction, and this cut does not
support one: the +0.010 is a coverage effect that **reverses** on the 125
records both arms answered (0.670 unconstrained against 0.667 constrained), and
the exact-call lead is 7 discordant records against 4 at p = 0.55. "Costs
nothing" is what the numbers support, and it is the interesting claim anyway —
a guarantee that is free is the result; a guarantee that pays for itself would
need evidence this cut does not have.

This was invisible with one dataset and is the strongest argument in this
report for evaluating constrained decoding across grammar *types*, not just
across benchmarks.
