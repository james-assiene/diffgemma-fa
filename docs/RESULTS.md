> ## Headline
>
> **Constraint satisfaction is exact, and with a grammar that admits the
> model's own rendering it is free.**
>
> | | CS | schema valid | arg acc | exact call |

> | unconstrained (stock model) | 0.000 | 0.631 | 0.641 | 0.469 |

> MAP emission, n = 130. The middle row is the result that made this look like
> constrained decoding costs half the accuracy; the bottom row is the same
> sampler against a grammar that does not forbid the model's newline+indent
> separators. Exact-call rate **exceeds** the unconstrained model, with every
> call a guaranteed-valid instance of the schema.
>
> **What is NOT shown here:** the paper reports constrained decoding *improving*
> accuracy (BFCL-Live 63.9 -> 71.5). We reproduce "no cost", not "a gain". The
> most likely missing piece is Mar confidence — recomputing the accept and
> stopping signals from the constrained marginals — which is implemented and
> reference-tested in `infer/marginals.py` but has never been called on the
> production path.

**Read the two leftmost columns together, and neither alone.**

- **CS** is the guarantee the sampler enforces: the emitted *token sequence* is accepted by the compiled automaton. That automaton carries SPEC §3.6's `<|channel>NAME\n<channel|>` header, which is **our** addition — the stock model has no reason to emit it, so the baseline arms score near zero here partly for a convention they were never asked to follow. A 0% → 100% CS jump reported on that basis alone would overstate the result.
- **schema valid** asks whether the parsed object validates against the schema. Header-independent and tokenizer-independent, so it is the question every arm can be asked fairly, and it is what the benchmark cares about.
- **arg acc** is per *argument*, over `arg_total` arguments rather than over records; **exact call** requires every argument of a call to be right and is the stricter, more comparable number.
- SPEC §3.8: CS can be trivially 100% while measuring nothing — a run emitting `{"location":""}` every time scores perfectly. That is why the content columns are never dropped.

## The grammar fix, and the ablation that explains it

The compiled grammar admitted **none** of the 130 outputs the unconstrained arm produced: it forbids the model's newline+indent separators, so at every value boundary the renormalised draw extends the value instead of closing it (`600` -> `6000`, `USA` -> `"USA1  "`). 58% of the measured accuracy gap was that. `--whitespace pretty` admits `( |\n {0,6})?`, the separators the model actually uses.

**The whitespace carries all of it.** On MAP emission at n=130, each component of the bundle alone: fence 0.328, case-insensitive enums 0.335, whitespace **0.622**, all three 0.628 — against a stock-grammar baseline of 0.328 and an unconstrained model at 0.641. The fence was motivated by a string-level proxy (verbatim acceptance of a whole unconstrained output: 0/130 without it, 74/130 with) that turned out not to predict the thing it was used to predict — mid-generation only the separators matter.

**float32 is not an option on the sample path.** It was briefly made the default on a toy-scale check (`L=4`, `|S|<=8`); at `L=256` on this grammar it drives the `Z == 0` detector on 70/130 and 55/130 records across two seeds, against 0/130 in float64. The guarantee held — those records fail loudly rather than emitting garbage — but the kernel is spuriously infeasible on half the corpus. The `exp_e5_*` artifacts are that measurement; the `exp_f64_*` ones are the float64 reruns.


## Coverage and caveats

- `unconstrained-sample`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 4859.5 s.
- `mask-sample`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 5767.9 s.
- `j2-sample`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 5726.0 s.
- `j1-sample`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 3685.5 s.
- `j0-map`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 3518.4 s.
- `j0-sample`: n=130 of 130 available; skipped at compile time: none; seed=0; entropy_bound=0.1; nonempty_strings=True; 5523.7 s.

- **Task**: `bfcl_live_simple`. This is one split, not all of BFCL. Records with more than one candidate function are excluded — the grammar is compiled from a single schema.
- `--nonempty` gives every required string `minLength: 1`. Measured cost: exactly two BFCL-Live ground truths are required strings the benchmark accepts as empty (`live_multiple_507-149-4`, `live_multiple_834-178-9`), and they are unanswerable under this flag.
- **`DGFA_DEDICATED=1`.** If 0, JAX preallocation is off and the GPU is shared: any latency number measured here is pessimistic and not publishable (CLAUDE.md). The columns above are accuracy, not latency, and are unaffected.
- **Only 130 of the 258 single-function records in this split were evaluated** — the first 130 by id. A prefix is not a random sample (BFCL ids cluster by schema family), so every number here is a first-half claim.
- Every arm is **one generation at one seed**. The measured seed spread for an identical config at n=30 is 0.412 / 0.412 / 0.314, so treat differences under ~0.1 at that size as noise.
- **SPEC §7.3's overhead table is not here and has never been measured.** That is the paper's headline performance claim (+4%), and it now has to be measured against a bandwidth-bound `log_matmul` rather than the GEMM the design assumed — the honest number may be materially worse.
- Missing from this table entirely: Spider, and five of the six datasets SPEC §7.1 lists. Not run. SPEC §3.8's refusal-branch union is unimplemented, so BFCL's 1,124 irrelevance records have no path to a score.
